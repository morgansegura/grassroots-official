# Scholarship Review Process

**Status: DRAFT for board adoption.** This document describes how Grassroots
Foundation receives, reviews, and decides need-based scholarship applications.
It is written to satisfy the private-benefit and conflict-of-interest
requirements that apply to a 501(c)(3). It has not been reviewed by counsel —
have a nonprofit attorney or CPA read it once before the first award, and record
its adoption in board minutes.

Last updated: August 19, 2026

---

## 1. Why this exists

A 501(c)(3) must serve a **charitable class** — a group broad and indefinite
enough that helping its members benefits the community, rather than a set of
pre-selected individuals. GRF's charitable class is:

> Youth in San Diego County whose households cannot absorb the cost of
> competitive club soccer.

Funding a member of that class is squarely within GRF's exempt purpose. The risk
is never _who_ receives an award — it is **how they were chosen**. An award made
because a board member knows the family, absent an open process, is a private
benefit regardless of how deserving the recipient is.

Everything below exists to make selection defensible: published criteria, an
open intake, an independent decision, and a written record.

## 2. Eligibility (published, applied uniformly)

An applicant is eligible when all of the following are true:

1. The player is a youth athlete in San Diego County.
2. The player plays, or is seeking to play, at a partner-club program.
3. Household financial need, as evidenced by household size and income relative
   to the current HHS Federal Poverty Guidelines, or by participation in a
   qualifying assistance program (free/reduced-price lunch, CalFresh, Medi-Cal,
   WIC, or similar).
4. The award would be applied to program costs — tuition, kit, equipment, or
   clinic fees.

**Not eligibility criteria:** athletic ability, tryout results, position, team
placement, or any relationship to GRF, its board, or its partner clubs.
Eligibility is need-based, never merit-based.

## 3. Intake

Two routes, both landing in the same queue:

- **Club referral** — a partner club's family-services contact identifies a
  family and refers them.
- **Direct application** — the family applies at `/apply`.

**No financial documents are collected at intake.** The application captures
household size and an income _bracket_, self-attested. This is deliberate:
storing pay stubs and tax returns creates a data-security and retention burden
disproportionate to a launch-year organization, and it is a barrier for
families. Documentation is verified later, and only for applications that
advance (§4, stage 3).

The form is available in English and Spanish. An intake that only speaks English
excludes part of the population GRF is chartered to serve.

## 4. Review

Applications move through a Monday board with these stages:

| Stage                      | What happens                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. New                     | Application received. Logged automatically.                                                                                                                                           |
| 2. Under review            | Reviewer checks eligibility (§2) against the published criteria.                                                                                                                      |
| 3. Documentation requested | If advancing: the partner club's family-services contact confirms enrollment and cost, and confirms need through their own process. GRF does not take custody of financial documents. |
| 4. Decision                | Approve (with amount) or decline, against criteria, recorded with a one-line rationale.                                                                                               |
| 5. Awarded / Declined      | Terminal. Applicant notified either way.                                                                                                                                              |

**Reviewers.** Every decision requires at least one reviewer who has no personal
relationship to the applicant. Where the board is small, the decision may be
delegated to a review committee that includes at least one non-board member.

**Timeline.** Applicants receive a decision within 10 business days of stage 2,
or an explanation of the delay. Publish this and hold to it.

**Capacity.** When demand exceeds funds, applications are prioritized by degree
of need, then by application date. Never by relationship, visibility, or team.

## 5. Conflict of interest and recusal

This is the section that matters most, and the one an examiner reads first.

A conflict exists whenever a reviewer, or a member of their household, has a
personal relationship with the applicant family — including, specifically, when
the player is a **teammate, clubmate, or classmate of the reviewer's own child**.

When a conflict exists, the conflicted person must:

1. **Disclose it in writing** before the application is discussed.
2. **Not advocate** for the applicant, formally or informally, to other
   reviewers.
3. **Not originate the referral.** A referral that traces back to the conflicted
   person is not arm's length. It must come from the club independently.
4. **Recuse from the decision** — no vote, and not present for the
   deliberation.
5. Have the recusal **recorded in the minutes** by name, with the nature of the
   conflict.

This applies to the founder and to every board member without exception. A
conflicted award is not made "carefully" — it is made by someone else.

## 6. Award and payment

- **Funds are paid to the partner club, never to the family.** This makes the
  award a payment for program services with a clean trail, rather than a gift to
  an individual.
- The award is applied to the named player's program costs for the named season.
- Awards are renewable, but renewal is a new application and a new decision.
- Every payment is recorded against the application record.

## 7. Records retained

For each application, GRF retains: the application, the eligibility
determination, the decision and rationale, any conflict disclosures and
recusals, and the payment record. Board minutes record the awards approved in
each period and any recusals.

These records are what make the program defensible on examination. They are also
what make it a real program rather than a series of individual favors.

## 8. Privacy and data handling

- Collected: applicant and player names, contact details, household size,
  income bracket, assistance-program participation, club, and a short
  description of need.
- **Not collected:** Social Security numbers, bank details, tax returns, pay
  stubs, or uploaded documents of any kind.
- Applications are visible only to reviewers.
- Applicants are told at intake who sees their application and why.
- See `/privacy` and `/donor-privacy` for the public-facing commitments.
