# Neighborhood Reinvestment Program: East County equipment grant

The full application package for a County of San Diego Neighborhood
Reinvestment Program (NRP) grant from Supervisor Joel Anderson's office
(District 2). It pays for soccer equipment for East County kids whose families
can't afford it. Jillian Osborne, the supervisor's Community Outreach Manager,
confirmed on November 4, 2026 that youth soccer equipment is eligible.

- **Where to submit:** the online form on
  [Supervisor Anderson's NRP page](https://www.supervisorjoelanderson.com/content/d2/us/en/grants/nrp.html).
  If the form fails, email everything to District2Grants@sdcounty.ca.gov.
- **When:** any time. Applications are rolling, and awards are made each
  quarter.
- **The form:** the county's application, Rev. 07/26, six pages. Download it
  from the page above.

Everything in **[brackets]** is a fact only Morgan can fill in.

## Before you submit: two things to settle

1. **A third director, before the board resolution.** The resolution
   (page 4 of the form) is adopted by the board. The bylaws and California law
   (Corporations Code §5212) require three directors, and Grass Roots
   Foundation has two of record (see `notes/governance-tracker.md`). Seat the
   third director at the reorganizing board meeting, and adopt the NRP
   resolution at the same meeting.
2. **The request has to fit the revenue cap.** The county's grant can't be
   more than 50% of the foundation's annual revenue, and the smallest request
   it accepts is $3,500. So the foundation needs at least $7,000 in revenue
   (this year's budget, or last year's actuals) to apply at all. The full
   request below ($8,630) needs about $17,300.
   - **Size the ask:** take half of this year's expected revenue, then round
     down to a whole number of kids at about $173 each: the set plus its share
     of gloves and replacement cleats.
   - **Ask Jillian which figure counts,** prior year or current budget, in the
     reply at the bottom of this file.

### Page 2: purpose of grant

> Copy this into the form.

Grass Roots Foundation (Grassroots Foundation) helps San Diego County kids
play soccer when their families can't afford it. We are asking for a
one-time grant to buy soccer equipment for 50 children in El Cajon,
Lakeside, and Santee whose families have shown financial need.

A season of club soccer costs a family far more than the fee. A child needs
cleats, shin guards, a ball, and a bag before the first practice, and growing
kids need new cleats partway through the season. For families stretched by
rent and groceries, that gear is often the reason a child stops playing.

Every item is a one-time purchase that goes directly to a child. In priority
order:

1. **Player equipment sets for 50 children, $7,450.** Cleats, shin guards, a
   size-appropriate ball, two pairs of socks, and a training bag, at $149
   per child.
2. **Goalkeeper gloves for 10 keepers, $400.** At $40 a pair.
3. **Growth replacement cleats for 12 children, $780.** For kids who outgrow
   or wear out their cleats during the season, so no one sits out because
   their shoes no longer fit.

Families apply through our confidential, need-based intake; there are no
tryouts and no merit requirement. We work with established clubs in the
community, so each child stays with their own team. Equipment is fitted and
handed out at a gear day at the start of the season, with the replacement
cleats held back for the season's second half. Grassroots Foundation
volunteers run the intake, the fitting, and the distribution, so every
grant dollar goes to equipment.

### Question 1: how we'll measure impact, and how many people we'll serve

> Copy this into the form.

We will serve 50 children, ages 6 to 18, and their families in El Cajon,
Lakeside, and Santee.

We measure four things and will report them to the Supervisor's office at the
end of the grant:

1. **Children equipped:** the number of children who received a full set,
   with their communities.
2. **Children still playing:** the share of equipped children still on their
   team at the end of the season. Our goal is 90%.
3. **Replacements filled:** growth-replacement cleats delivered within two
   weeks of a family's request.
4. **What families tell us:** a short survey at season's end asking whether
   the equipment made it possible for their child to keep playing.
