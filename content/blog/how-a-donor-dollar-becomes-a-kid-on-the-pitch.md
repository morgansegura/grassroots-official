This is a piece about _exactly_ what happens to a donor dollar between the moment it is given and the moment a kid steps onto a pitch wearing kit funded by it.

It is not a piece about general impact. It is not a piece about heart-warming outcomes. It is a piece about mechanics, written for the donor who wants to know how the money actually moves before they give.

## The framework

Most nonprofits — when they're being honest — operate against a chain that looks like this:

**Input → Activity → Output → Outcome → Impact**

- **Input** is the donor dollar.
- **Activity** is the work the nonprofit does with it.
- **Output** is the immediate, countable thing the activity produces.
- **Outcome** is the change in the world the output enables.
- **Impact** is the long-term, harder-to-measure effect.

Most nonprofit storytelling collapses these into one. _"Your gift transforms lives."_ That's a sentence about impact attached to an input number, with everything in between hidden. We are going to walk every step.

## Step 1 — Input

Your gift enters Grassroots Foundation through one of two channels:

- **Zeffy** for one-time and recurring gifts. Zeffy charges zero platform fees to nonprofits in the U.S., funded by a tipping model that's optional for the donor.
- **PayPal Giving Fund** for PayPal-originated gifts and DAF (donor-advised fund) routing where the donor's DAF supports the PayPal Giving Fund pathway.

Both channels deposit gifts directly into the foundation's operating account. We do not handle credit-card data, we don't store payment information, and the donor receives an automated receipt from the processor for tax purposes.

What you give is what we receive. **Processing-fee leakage is minimal** — typically below 1% of gross at Zeffy's structure — and we don't take a cut of named-program gifts to cover unrelated overhead.

## Step 2 — Activity

Once the gift lands, it is allocated against one of five programs:

- **Player Scholarships** — tuition coverage at partner clubs.
- **Team Sponsorship** — full-roster underwrite at a specific competitive tier.
- **Kit & Uniform Grants** — match-day kit, training kit, sideline gear.
- **Equipment Grants** — cleats, ball, shin guards, goalkeeper gear, training bag.
- **Methodology Clinic Access** — reserved free seats at methodology training clinics.

You can direct your gift to a specific program at the point of giving, or you can give to the general fund. General-fund dollars are allocated to whichever program has the largest open gap at the time of the next cohort placement.

Operational activity inside this step includes:

- **Confidential family income verification** with partner clubs, so the kid's intake is need-based and documented.
- **Partner-club invoicing** — we receive the tuition or kit invoice from the partner club and pay directly. The family does not handle the dollars.
- **Direct equipment fulfillment** for items we don't route through clubs — typically goalkeeper kits and growth-spurt mid-season replacements.

## Step 3 — Output

Output is the countable thing produced by the activity. For Grassroots Foundation, the outputs are:

- **Scholarship placements.** A kid funded for a competitive season at a partner club. The unit is one kid for one season.
- **Kit and equipment fulfillments.** Direct deliveries to a specific kid for a specific season.
- **Reserved clinic seats.** A scholarship-recipient seat held at a methodology training clinic.

These are auditable. We can tell you, at any reasonable cadence, how many scholarship placements have been funded, how many kits delivered, how many clinic seats reserved. When the foundation publishes its first audited financial year, these numbers will be footnoted in the program-expense breakdown.

For Class of '26 specifically, the target output is **100 funded scholarship placements** across San Diego County by the start of the 2026 competitive season. The first 25 — the founding-donor block — are funded by donors who give before kickoff.

## Step 4 — Outcome

Outcome is what the output produces in the world. For scholarship placements, the outcomes we track are:

- **Roster continuity.** Did the kid stay on the roster through the full season the scholarship covered?
- **Re-enrollment.** Did the kid return the following season — either on the original scholarship (renewed) or on the partner club's standard fee structure?
- **Developmental progression.** Did the kid advance to the next age band or competitive tier within the partner club's pathway?

These are measurable, and we will measure them. They are also slower to land than the outputs are — the first full season's outcome data won't be visible until the 2026–27 cycle reports out in spring 2027.

We will not be reporting on outcomes we don't yet have. If a future blog post claims a 90% roster-continuity rate, the underlying data will be linked.

## Step 5 — Impact

Impact is the long-term, harder-to-measure effect. For Grassroots Foundation, the impacts we believe scholarship placement contributes to are:

- **Talent pool widening.** More kids from working-class San Diego households remaining in the competitive developmental pathway through the U12-to-U14 drop-off window.
- **Closing the geography gap.** South Bay and East County representation on competitive rosters that historically skew toward higher-income parts of the county.
- **Demonstrating a model.** A repeatable, auditable approach to closing the U.S. pay-to-play cost wall that other organizations can adopt in other regions.

Impact at this level is multi-year and partially attributable. We will not claim, at any point, that one donor dollar measurably moved any of these aggregate-level numbers. We will claim that it moved one kid through one season — and that aggregate impact, over time, is what those individual moves add up to.

## A worked example

A donor gives **$2,500** to Player Scholarships in May. Here is what happens:

- **Day 0:** Gift hits the operating account via Zeffy.
- **Day 1–5:** Receipted automatically. Allocated against the Class of '26 Player Scholarships line.
- **Week 6–10:** Partner-club intake cycle. Family income verified. Kid placed on a competitive roster at a partner club for the upcoming season.
- **Week 12:** Invoice from partner club. Foundation pays tuition directly. **The $2,500 covers one kid for one season.**
- **Season start (late summer):** Kid begins competitive play on the partner-club roster. We send the donor a notification that the placement has happened and which program funded it. If the family consents to recognition, the donor is named as a Class of '26 founding donor.
- **Mid-season:** Kit and equipment reviewed. Any growth-spurt mid-season replacement is funded out of Equipment Grants, not the original $2,500.
- **End of season:** Outcome data captured. Roster continuity, re-enrollment, and developmental progression noted in the foundation's internal records.
- **Following spring:** Audited financial year reports out. Donor receives a year-end statement and a high-level breakdown of where the cohort sits.

That's the path. Every step is named. Every step is auditable. Every step is something we will be able to show the donor if they ask.

## What we won't do

A few specifics, because they matter:

- We won't fabricate outcome data ahead of when the season produces it.
- We won't show progress bars on campaign pages that reflect anything other than real dollars in the account.
- We won't attribute aggregate impact to specific donor dollars when the dollars are pooled across a cohort.
- We won't sell or share donor information with any third party.
- We won't bundle general-fund dollars into specific-program reporting.

## The honest read

This is what philanthropic transparency actually looks like at the line level. It is not glamorous. It is not "your gift transforms lives." It is a chain that moves a specific dollar amount from a specific donor to a specific kid on a specific partner-club roster for a specific season — with every step in between named, dated, and auditable.

If that's what you want from a place to give, the path begins here.
