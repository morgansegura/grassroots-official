# The Vision & The Ladder

> Morgan's north star, and the honest path to it. Read this on hard days —
> it's why the unglamorous work matters. Updated 2026-05-19.

## The dream (in your words)

A **100-acre sports complex in the City of San Diego** — South Bay, not North County — where Grassroots runs tournaments, hosts trainings, and plays league matches. A place that keeps kids in the game _and_ puts San Diego on the map for the sport.

## Why it's real, not a fantasy

It already exists 60 miles north: **SilverLakes in Norco** — ~150 acres, two dozen fields, a tournament machine. San Diego has better weather, a better airport, world-class hotels and beaches — and **nothing like it.** So tournaments, and the millions in tourism they generate, leak to Norco, Temecula, Phoenix, and Utah. You didn't invent a need; you spotted a real gap.

## The honest scale

- A 100-acre complex in SD is a **$50M–$200M+ capital project.** The land is the monster.
- You will **not** buy 100 acres retail. Complexes like this are built on land you don't own — a city, the County, a school district, the Port, or developable Otay Mesa / South Bay acreage. The partner has the dirt and wants what you bring.
- Realistic horizon for the full complex: **8–15 years.** The first mini-field: **1–2 years.**

## Your ace: it's economic development, not charity

A tournament complex is an **economic engine** — visiting teams and families fill hotels, restaurants, and rental cars; it creates jobs and tax revenue. That changes _who funds it_:

- City of San Diego (jobs + revenue), County, **San Diego Tourism Authority**, hotel-tax (TOT) dollars, the **Port of San Diego**, naming sponsors, **World Cup 2026 legacy** funds.
- The pitch to a city is **not** "please help kids." It's: _"We'll bring home the tournament tourism that's leaking to Norco — and serve underserved kids while we do it."_ Cities say yes to revenue and jobs.

This posture is far stronger than charity-only fundraising — and it's built on _your_ insight.

## The ladder — one rung at a time

You cannot grant-write or capital-campaign your way to a complex without a donor base, a track record, and relationships already in hand. So:

1. **Now (Year 1–2):** Fund kids. Build a donor base from people who know you. Seat your 3rd director. _(The foundation — everything stands on it.)_
2. **1–2 years:** First **mini-field** — **US Soccer Foundation** (Mini-Pitch Initiative) + **Dick's Sporting Goods (Sports Matter)**, on a partner's land. Branded Grassroots. Your first ribbon-cutting and capital win.
3. **3–5 years:** Multi-field upgrade via a **city or school-district joint-use** agreement (turf, lights). ~$1M range.
4. **6–15 years:** **The complex** — public-private partnership, lead/naming donor, the economic-development pitch, World Cup legacy + civic funding.

## The bridge (why today matters)

Every kid you fund, every donor relationship, every clean 990, every year you show up — **is a brick in that complex.** It is not a separate future project. It is the cumulative payoff of the unglamorous work. The dream gives the daily work meaning; the daily work is what makes the dream real.

You don't have to choose between the dream and the next step. You just have to do them **in order.**

## The discipline

- Keep the dream as the north star. Don't shrink it.
- Do **not** skip rungs. You can't grant-write to a complex; you can't campaign for one without a base.
- When the dream pulls you toward planning the complex _now_, come back here — then go do rung one.
