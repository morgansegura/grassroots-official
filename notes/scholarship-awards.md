# Scholarship Awards — How to Give One Cleanly

> Follow this every time you award a scholarship. It keeps awards mission-driven,
> IRS-clean, and free of conflict-of-interest problems. Updated 2026-05-19.

## The one hard rule: no insiders' kids

Do **not** fund the child of an insider — your own kids, a relative, or the child of any director/officer. Using charitable dollars to cover a fee an insider would otherwise pay personally is _private benefit / inurement_, and it can threaten your 501(c)(3) status. This holds even though your family is in the soccer world.

## Knowing the kid is fine

Funding a kid you personally know — a neighborhood kid, a kid from a team, a family friend who isn't on your board — is **not** a conflict of interest. A small community nonprofit is _supposed_ to know the kids it serves. What matters is the process below, not whether you know them.

## The three safeguards (do all three, every time)

1. **Objective, need-based eligibility.** The kid qualifies on documented need, not on relationship. Capture: family situation / income basis, the club + actual fee, age / team / program.
2. **Document the decision.** A one-page award record (template below). Note any relationship to an insider — if one exists, that insider recuses from the decision.
3. **Pay the club directly — never the family.** The money buys tuition; it does not pass through anyone's hands. This is the single biggest protection.

Do those three and "a kid we know can't afford it" becomes "an applicant met our published criteria, documented." That's airtight, and it's exactly how community scholarships are meant to work.

## Award record — copy this per kid

```
SCHOLARSHIP AWARD
Recipient: ____________________
Need basis: ____________________
Club / program: ____________________   Fee: $______
Amount awarded: $______   Paid to (club): ____________________
Date: ______   Approved by: ______
Relationship to any insider?  ☐ none  ☐ yes → who recused: ______
Consent to share story (name/photo)?  ☐ yes  ☐ no
```

## Capture the story (with consent)

For every funded kid, _with the family's written consent_: a name, a photo, a coach's note, the family's words. This is your most valuable fundraising asset — it powers personal asks and, over time, grant applications. No consent = keep it private, no public use.

## When the board forms

Adopt the **Conflict of Interest policy** and these eligibility criteria formally (Guide Ch. 8). Until then, the three safeguards above keep you clean. Don't wait on the board to fund your first kid — just don't fund a relative, and document the need.

_Not legal advice — for edge cases, your COI policy + a nonprofit attorney settle it. The rule above covers ~95% of situations._
