# How to Ask — for someone great with people, new to the ask

> Read this before you pick up the phone. Short on purpose. Reread as needed.

## The truth that changes everything

You are not bad at fundraising. Fundraising is **95% relationship, 5% the ask.** The relationship part — trust, listening, making people feel seen, caring for real — can't be taught, and you already have it. The ask is a small, learnable skill. You're nervous about the most coachable 30 seconds of the entire process, while you've already mastered the hard part most people never get.

## Three reframes that dissolve the fear

**1. You're not asking for money — you're inviting someone to do something meaningful with theirs.** The money doesn't go to you; it puts a kid on the field. You're offering a person who trusts you the chance to change a life. That's a gift you _give_ them, not a favor you beg for. Relationship people freeze because they imagine asking = taking. It's the opposite.

**2. Specific is kind; vague is the cruel one.** "Would you support us?" makes the donor do all the work — how much? for what? Hard to answer, easy to defer. "Would you fund one kid for a season — $2,500?" is a clean question they can answer with dignity. Specificity respects people.

**3. Make the ask, then stop talking.** The one mechanical trick that matters. After you ask, be silent. Let them answer. Don't rescue them with "…but no pressure, only if you can, totally fine if not" — that talks them right out of it. Ask. Then quiet. Whoever speaks first loses.

## A simple script (make it yours)

1. **Reconnect** — the part you're already great at. Catch up. Be a human, not a campaign.
2. **Open the door:** _"Can I tell you about something I've been pouring myself into?"_
3. **The story — one kid, one truth.** Not statistics. _"There's a kid in [neighborhood] whose family can't cover the club fee. He's good enough to make the team — he just can't afford to be on it. We exist so kids like him don't get cut for the wrong reason."_
4. **The specific invite:** _"I'm looking for [N] people to each fund one kid for a season — $2,500. Would you be one of them?"_
5. **Stop. Let them answer.**

## What to say when they say…

- **Yes:** _"That means more than you know — you just put a kid on the field. Let me show you exactly who."_ Then steward: thank-you within 48 hours, and the kid's update/photo when you have it.
- **Maybe / "let me think":** _"Of course. Can I follow up next week?"_ Then actually follow up. Most maybes become yeses with one gentle nudge.
- **No / not now:** _"I completely understand. Can I keep you in the loop as we grow?"_ Grace. A no today is often a yes in a year.

## The deep breath

A "no" is never a rejection of _you_ — it's "not this" or "not now." Done with grace, an ask doesn't cost you a relationship; it deepens it, because you trusted them enough to invite them in.

You have the gift. The ask is just the last 30 seconds — and 30 seconds is learnable.
