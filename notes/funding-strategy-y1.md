# Funding strategy — Year 1 playbook

The strategic plan (v1) sets the destination: **$175K raised in Y1, ~75 kids
funded, public-support test cleared, foundation pipeline opened.** This doc
is the execution layer — what you and your wife actually do, week by week,
to land that number.

It assumes you've never run a nonprofit fundraising operation before. It is
opinionated, not exhaustive. Push back where the numbers don't fit your
reality — but the structure (mix, cadence, sequencing) holds.

---

## 1. What "raising $175K" actually looks like

Per the strategic plan, Year 1 revenue is split across five channels:

| Channel                                   | Y1 target | What it actually means              |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------: | ----------------------------------- |
| Individual small gifts                    |      $35K | ~350 donors averaging $100          |
| First Touch sustainers                    |      $15K | ~25 monthly donors averaging $50/mo |
| Major gifts + team sponsorships           |      $50K | 3–8 gifts in the $5K–$25K range     |
| Foundation grants                         |      $50K | 2–4 awards in the $10K–$25K range   |
| Corporate / matching / PayPal / Goodstack |      $25K | passive yield + 1–2 corporate asks  |
| **Total**                                 | **$175K** |                                     |

Two important properties of this mix:

1. **It is diversified.** No single donor is ~25% of revenue. That's the
   public-support test (33%) cleared with margin. Foundation officers and
   the IRS Schedule A screen for this — design the mix this way on purpose.
2. **Individual + sustainer + major-gift = $100K, or 57% of revenue.** This
   is the part you control directly through outreach. The grants ($50K) and
   passive yield ($25K) are slower and partly out of your hands. **The
   fundraising work is mostly individual donor work.**

If the only thing you took from this doc was "57% of Year 1 comes from
asking people I know," that would be enough.

## 2. The donor pyramid you're building

Most first-time founders chase volume at the bottom and ignore the top.
This is backwards — the top tier is fewer people but more dollars and far
less work per dollar. Build the pyramid from the top down.

```
                          ┌────────────────────┐
                          │   $25K+  (1–2)    │   ← team sponsorships
                          └────────────────────┘
                       ┌──────────────────────────┐
                       │   $5K–$25K  (3–6)        │   ← major individual gifts
                       └──────────────────────────┘
                    ┌────────────────────────────────┐
                    │   $1K–$5K   (15–25)            │   ← mid-tier individuals
                    └────────────────────────────────┘
                 ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
                 │   $100–$1K  (75–125)                 │   ← committed individuals
                 └──────────────────────────────────────┘
              ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
              │   $25–$100  (200–300)                      │   ← founding-donor base
              └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
              ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
              │   First Touch sustainers (25)              │   ← $50/mo recurring
              └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Approximate counts for Y1:**

| Tier                  |           Avg gift | Donors needed |  Sub-total | Source                          |
| --------------------- | -----------------: | ------------: | ---------: | ------------------------------- |
| Team sponsor          |            $25,000 |             2 |       $50K | Major-gift outreach             |
| Major individual      |             $7,500 |             5 |     $37.5K | Founders' inner network + board |
| Mid-tier              |             $2,000 |            15 |       $30K | Founders' broader network       |
| Committed individual  |               $300 |            75 |     $22.5K | P2P + sphere extension          |
| Founding-donor base   |                $50 |           300 |       $15K | Social + cold + appeals         |
| First Touch sustainer | $50/mo × 12 = $600 |            25 |       $15K | Conversion from above tiers     |
| **Total**             |                    |      **~422** | **~$170K** |                                 |

This rounds to your $175K target. The numbers should feel ambitious but
not insane — and they suggest the right number of _real conversations_ you
need to have (not the right number of social posts).

**Mental model:** every tier on this pyramid is a different _kind_ of
conversation. A $50 gift comes from a text. A $7,500 gift comes from a
30-minute coffee. A $25K team sponsor comes from a multi-touch relationship
over weeks. Don't try to ask everyone the same way.

## 3. The 90-day plan

Front-loaded for one reason: **the founder's network is the highest-yield
fundraising asset Year 1 will ever have.** It depreciates the moment you
launch (people only get the "this is new — be part of the founding!" pitch
once). Use it now.

### Days 1–30 — Infrastructure + first 10 founding donors

**Goals this month:**

- Donate flow tested end-to-end
- One-pager written, designed, PDF + public link
- 50-person network list built
- **First 10 founding donors on the board**
- $5K–$10K raised

**This week:**

- [ ] Test donate flow on phone + desktop. Make a $25 gift to yourself.
      Confirm receipt email lands. Confirm the Zeffy dashboard shows it
      and that the Zeffy tip prompt is shown to the donor (your gift
      lands at 100% — donors are asked optionally to tip Zeffy).
      Confirm the Class of '26 progress number reflects it on the site
      (manually for now, eventually via webhook).
- [ ] Build the **one-pager** (see §6). One page, PDF + public link.
- [ ] List your 50 people. Categorize by capacity (Capacity tier columns:
      $50–$100 / $500–$2,500 / $5K+). Estimate honestly. Save as a working
      doc — this is your CRM until you outgrow a spreadsheet.

**Next two weeks:**

- [ ] Send 10 personal asks. Text or call — **not email**. Script in §6.
      Goal: 5–8 yeses, $2K–$5K. Real money.
- [ ] Each donor gets a same-day thank-you. Hand-typed, by name.
      Mention their kid / their connection / why this matters to them
      specifically. Stewardship starts at gift #1.

### Days 31–60 — Founding cohort push

**Goals this month:**

- 25 founding donors total ($25K raised)
- First 5 First Touch sustainers signed up
- Board formed if it isn't yet (3 members minimum)
- CA Franchise Tax Board good standing confirmed
- Small grant applications drafted (LA84 small, Good Sports)

**Actions:**

- [ ] Continue working the 50-person list. Ask the next 20.
- [ ] At gift #15, send a **"founding cohort" update email** to all donors
      with: progress to roster of 100, the first scholarship recipient if
      you have one (or honest "not yet — funding now"), and a P2P ask
      (would you forward this to 3 people who might care?).
- [ ] Apply for **Good Sports** equipment grant. No money in, kits/cleats
      out — easiest win.
- [ ] Draft (don't yet submit) the **LA84 small grant**. Have it ready for
      Day 60.
- [ ] Sit with someone for coffee in each $5K+ row of your list. **Don't
      ask for money in the first conversation.** Ask for advice, for
      introductions, for them to sit on the board, for them to make calls
      with you. The money ask comes meeting 2 or 3.
- [ ] Set up Double the Donation widget on /donate (employer match).

### Days 61–90 — Sustainers + first grants out

**Goals this month:**

- 40 founding donors total ($40K–$50K raised)
- 10–15 First Touch sustainers
- 2 small grants submitted
- 1–2 major-gift conversations in progress
- First scholarship recipient if possible (real story for fundraising
  collateral going forward)

**Actions:**

- [ ] Submit LA84 small grant.
- [ ] Submit San Diego Foundation application (referencing the SDFC
      $100K pool — they know about it; mention it explicitly).
- [ ] Run a **First Touch launch** — dedicated email and social campaign
      asking existing donors to switch from one-time to monthly. Frame:
      "$50/mo = one kid for one year." Convert at least 10 of the existing
      40 into monthly.
- [ ] Have the first 1–2 major-gift conversations move to ask stage.
- [ ] If a scholarship has actually been placed by now (a real kid funded
      at a partner club): get family written consent, do a small write-up,
      publish (with photo only if explicit signed media release).

By Day 90 you should be at: **$50K–$75K raised, 40+ donors, 10–15
sustainers, 2 grants in progress, the SDFC relationship opened, and ready
to apply for LA84's July 21 large grant cycle.**

## 4. Channel playbooks

Each channel has different physics. Don't treat them the same.

### 4a. Individual giving (sphere of influence) — Y1 lead channel

**Mental model:** every person you've ever liked or worked with is a
potential donor at _some_ dollar amount. Your job is to figure out which
amount and ask for it specifically.

**Acquisition cost:** ~$0. No paid ads in Year 1. (Paid acquisition for
individual donors usually costs $0.50–$2 per dollar raised — you can't
afford it yet.)

**Tactics:**

- **Personal outreach in the first 90 days.** Text > call > email. Volume
  is unimportant; relationship strength is everything.
- **Year-end appeal in November–December.** ~30–40% of all annual
  individual giving lands in December. Plan for this in October. Email +
  one social post — not 12. Keep it tight.
- **Birthday / anniversary asks from your donors.** People raise hundreds
  of dollars on their birthdays for orgs they love. Zeffy supports
  peer-to-peer campaigns natively at 0% fee — every birthday P2P is
  pure margin. Encourage it after a donor gives twice.
- **Stewardship first, asking second.** Donor lifetime value > first gift.
  A $50 first-time donor who gives every year for 5 years is worth more
  than a $200 one-and-done.

**Retention:** target 65%+ donor retention into Y2. Industry average is
~45% — anything above 60% is good for a young org and signals the
fundraising mechanics are healthy.

### 4b. First Touch sustainers — highest leverage channel

**Why this matters more than anything:** a $50/mo sustainer is worth more
than a $500 one-time gift because (a) annual value is $600, (b) average
sustainer retention is 80%+ year-over-year, and (c) they reduce your
fundraising labor every subsequent year.

**Y1 target: 25 sustainers.** Cumulative annual value = $15K. By Y3 these
25 sustainers are still ~20 (with churn), with new sustainers stacked on
top — that's the compounding engine.

**Recruitment:**

- Default ask amount on the donate page: $50/month, framed as "$50 funds
  one kid for one season."
- **Convert from one-time donors.** Every one-time donor's thank-you email
  includes a soft sustainer ask 30 days later. Build this into the
  receipt/follow-up cadence on the Zeffy side.
- **Use the World Cup tailwind.** "Become a First Touch sustainer before
  the World Cup — your gift funds the Class of '26."
- **Recognition with no ceremony.** First Touch sustainers get quarterly
  updates by name, are invited to clinic events, and get a small token
  (kit-branded sticker, signed photo from a clinic — once you have them).

**Don't:** offer premium tiers (gold/silver) — adds complexity, doesn't
move the needle at Y1 scale. Single tier ($50/mo). Add custom amount
field.

### 4c. Major gifts — slowest channel, highest yield per hour

**Mental model:** a major gift is a _yes_ you've earned by building a
relationship over weeks. The check is the _last_ event, not the first.

**Sequence for any major-gift donor:**

1. **Discovery (meeting 1):** coffee, no ask. Get to know them, listen
   for the cause hook ("my daughter aged out of soccer because of cost"
   is the hook you're listening for). Talk about Grassroots when asked.
   End with: "I'd love to send you our one-pager — can I follow up?"
2. **Inform (week 1–3):** send one-pager + a personal note. No ask.
   Maybe a media reference (a Times of San Diego piece, a relevant
   article on the dropoff window).
3. **Involve (meeting 2):** specific moment that draws them in. Could
   be: meet the founders, attend a clinic, introduce them to a partner
   club director. Not a fundraising event — an experience.
4. **Ask (meeting 3 or 4):** specific dollar amount, specific use,
   specific timeline. "I'd like to ask you to consider a $10,000 gift
   to sponsor four scholarship recipients in our Class of '26 cohort.
   You'd be one of our first major donors, and your name would be the
   first on the founding-donor list we publish quarterly."
5. **Thank (within 24 hrs of yes):** handwritten note. Phone call from
   you AND your wife. Public acknowledgment if they consent. Private
   acknowledgment if they don't.
6. **Steward (ongoing):** quarterly updates, invitation to the next
   clinic, personalized "your gift funded X" follow-up.

**Pitfalls:**

- **Asking too soon.** First-time founders ask in meeting 1. The yes rate
  drops by ~70% vs asking in meeting 3.
- **Asking too small.** Anchor high (specific dollar amount, justified by
  unit cost). People give what you ask for — your number is the ceiling.
- **Not knowing the capacity.** Look up gift history at other orgs via
  Candid Foundation Directory or quiet network research. People give in
  patterns.

### 4d. Foundation grants — Y2 main channel; Y1 small-grant only

**Year 1 reality:** without a filed 990, audited financials, or
demonstrated program execution, you can apply to small grants only ($5K–
$25K range). Apply broadly to these; don't waste cycles on $100K+ proposals
that won't land for a Y1 org.

**Y1 target: 2–4 small grants × $10K–$25K = $20K–$80K**

**Priority order (per `grants-funding-landscape.md`):**

| Funder                                         |              Award range | Status              | When to apply                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -----------------------: | ------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Good Sports                                    | In-kind (kits/equipment) | Rolling             | Week 1                           |
| LA84 — Small grant                             |                 $5K–$25K | Rolling             | Day 45–60                        |
| San Diego Foundation                           |                   Varies | Cycle-based         | Day 60–90 (reference SDFC pool)  |
| LA84 — Large grant                             |                    $25K+ | July 21 deadline    | Day 60 draft, submit by deadline |
| U.S. Soccer Foundation (next Innovate to Grow) |                $20K–$50K | Watch               | Year 1–2                         |
| Conrad Prebys Foundation                       |                   Varies | Relationship-driven | LOI Day 90, formal Y2            |

**One-pager you'll attach to every grant application:**

- Mission statement (1 sentence)
- Programs (1–2 sentences each)
- Year 1 budget summary (~$175K)
- Program-to-funding match (which grants fund which programs)
- Roster of 100 progress + photo
- Contact info

Most grant applications are 80% boilerplate + 20% funder-specific
customization. Build the boilerplate once.

### 4e. Corporate matching, DAF, PayPal Giving Fund — passive + active

**Passive (already running):**

- Zeffy — the entire donate flow runs through here. Donations flow at
  **0% platform fee**; Zeffy makes its money from optional donor tips.
  At a $175K Y1 target this saves ~$5K–$9K vs Givebutter/Stripe-direct.
  That's the difference between funding two extra kids and not.
- Goodstack — donations flow through. ~$5K–$10K likely from existing
  donors who happen to be on Goodstack-supported platforms. No active
  work.
- PayPal Giving Fund — same. ~$2K–$5K likely.

**Active:**

- **Double the Donation widget** on /donate. Costs <$300/year, surfaces
  the donor's employer match eligibility at gift time. Adds 10–30% to
  individual giving totals at near-zero effort. Install in Month 1.
- **DAF widget** (Chariot or DAFpay). Costs $0 if Chariot. Adds a "give
  through your donor-advised fund" option. High-net-worth donors often
  have DAF balances they want to deploy — surfacing this turns "I'll
  think about it" into "actually I have $5K parked in my DAF."
- **Direct corporate asks** — 1–2 in Y1. Frame: "Sponsor 10 scholarship
  recipients for the Class of '26 — $25,000 commitment, your logo on the
  roster page, recognition in our annual report and at our launch clinic."
  Target SD-headquartered companies whose CSR aligns: Sempra (energy +
  community), Qualcomm Foundation, Petco Love (animal-focused — likely
  not a fit), Illumina (limited CSR), Sycuan Casino (tribal community
  giving — strong fit), Viejas (same).

## 5. The numbers you need to be tracking

A weekly Friday-afternoon dashboard. No CRM yet — Google Sheet or Airtable.

### Weekly snapshot (5 metrics)

1. **Donors acquired this week** (new + recurring)
2. **Dollars raised this week**
3. **Cumulative $ raised this year** vs the $175K target
4. **First Touch sustainers active** vs the 25 target
5. **Pipeline conversations** (people in major-gift or grant pipeline)

### Monthly snapshot (add 4 more)

6. **Donor retention** (cohort: gave 12+ months ago, gave this month → %)
7. **Average gift size** by tier
8. **Cost per dollar raised** (everything you spend on fundraising / $
   raised — target: <$0.20 per $1 in Y1, <$0.15 by Y3)
9. **% to programs** (target: >75% — this is the foundation officer screen)

### Quarterly snapshot (add 3 more)

10. **Major-gift pipeline value** (sum of dollar potential across all
    pipeline conversations × your gut probability)
11. **Foundation pipeline value** (same math for grants in flight)
12. **Class of '26 progress** — kids actually funded

Don't over-instrument. These twelve metrics, reviewed religiously, will
keep you ahead of 80% of first-time nonprofit operators.

## 7. The asks framework

How you ask matters as much as who you ask. A few proven patterns.

### Cold or warm intro — $25 to $500 (text or short message)

> Hey [name] — quick one. My wife and I launched a 501(c)(3) called
> Grassroots Foundation that funds youth soccer scholarships for working-
> class San Diego kids who'd otherwise get priced out around age 11–13.
> Our goal is to put 100 kids on a roster by World Cup 2026.
>
> I'm reaching out to people I trust to be among our first 25 founding
> donors. Any chance you'd consider a $50/month sustainer gift, or a
> one-time gift at whatever level works? Link here: [onepager + donate].
>
> Even if not — I'd love to send you the one-pager so you know what
> we're up to.

Why this works: states relationship, says what the org is, names the
goal, makes a specific ask, leaves dignity in a no. Read it out loud
before sending — should sound like you.

### Coffee for a higher-capacity person ($2.5K–$10K range)

Don't ask in the first meeting. Discovery first. End with:

> I'd love to keep you in the loop — would it be okay if I sent you our
> one-pager and a short update in a few weeks?

Second meeting, after you've sent the one-pager and a follow-up:

> [Name], you've been generous with your time and feedback. I want to
> ask you something specific. I'm building our founding-donor cohort —
> the first 25 donors who'll be on the wall of the org forever as the
> ones who got it started. Would you consider a $5,000 founding gift?
> It funds two kids for a full season — Class of '26 recipients. Your
> name on the founding wall, your decision on which two players (if you
> want), and quarterly updates on their progress through the cohort.

Why this works: specific ask, specific dollar, specific outcome,
specific recognition. Anchored to the unit cost. Names the founder
cohort frame. Gives them a no-friction yes.

### Major-gift / team-sponsor ask ($25K+)

Always in person. Two founders in the meeting if you can manage it
(you + your wife). The ask:

> [Name], we'd like to ask you to consider sponsoring a full team for
> the inaugural Class of '26 cohort. That's $25,000 — one full season,
> 16–20 kids who'd otherwise be priced out, every kid plays. Your name
> on the roster page, recognition at our launch clinic, and you'd be
> our first team sponsor — which carries weight when we ask the next
> ten foundations and corporate partners to follow.

If they hesitate: don't fill the silence. Let them think. Don't
discount yourself by offering a smaller amount. If they push back,
the next move is "what feels right to you?" — not "would $10K work?"

### Foundation grant LOI (LA84 example)

Don't pitch yourself as a grantmaker (the "are we doing the same thing"
trap from the last conversation). Pitch yourself as:

> Grassroots Foundation is the intake-and-distribution layer for need-
> qualified youth soccer scholarships in San Diego County. We do not
> operate soccer programs — we fund individual kids into existing
> partner-club programs at every competitive tier (community, NPL, GA,
> MLS Next, ECNL). Our value-add to the youth-sports funding ecosystem
> is (1) intake and eligibility administration partner clubs lack the
> staff to operate, (2) whole-cost-stack coverage — tuition, kit,
> equipment, travel, clinic seats — where internal club scholarships
> only cover tuition, and (3) kid-level outcome tracking through the
> 11–13 dropoff window. A $20,000 grant from LA84 would fund eight
> scholarship recipients in our inaugural Class of '26 cohort, with
> documented kid-level outcomes reported quarterly.

Why this works: explicitly distinguishes from "we redistribute money" to
"we operate the distribution machinery you can't operate yourself." Names
specific value-add. Specific dollar ask tied to specific outcomes.

## 8. What NOT to do in Year 1

These are the most common Year 1 mistakes. Avoid them.

- **No paid digital advertising.** Costs $0.50–$2.00 per dollar raised
  at your scale. You don't have the data to optimize. Comes later.
- **No galas, no events with venue/catering cost.** Most Year 1 events
  net negative on cash. The exception: a clinic event with photographer
  (already in the strategic plan) — that's program work that doubles as
  fundraising collateral, not an event built for fundraising.
- **No major fundraising hire.** You're the fundraiser. Hiring before
  you've personally done the work means you don't know what success
  looks like, and you can't manage to it.
- **No premature large grants ($100K+).** Without track record, you'll
  get politely declined and burn 3 months. Apply for $5K–$25K small
  grants instead. Larger grants come Y2.
- **No "letter campaign" to 500 strangers.** Direct mail to cold lists
  is a high-skill, high-cost channel. Year 1 doesn't have the budget or
  audience size to make it work.
- **No premature board expansion.** 3–5 board members in Y1 is correct
  — chosen for fundraising capacity, governance discipline, and
  community standing. Growth comes Y2–Y3.
- **No "I'll fundraise once the site is perfect."** The site is a tool,
  not a destination. The site is good enough now (you just launched).
  Make the asks.

That last one is the trap most operator-founders fall into. Naming it
explicitly so you can spot it in yourself: **if you find yourself
adding features instead of making calls, the calls are the harder thing
and the right thing.**
