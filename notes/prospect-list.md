---

## The target

**5–15 people who could write $1K–$50K _if they fully believed in this_**.

Not "would write today." _Could_, if asked properly. These are people who would already take your phone call — not cold prospects, not strangers, not internet leads.

This is the work that 80% of nonprofit money comes from. Five people giving $25K beats 500 people giving $50.

---

## The mindset (read before filling in)

Donors expect to be asked. The people who care about you, and care about kids in San Diego who can't afford to play, _want_ to be on this list. You're not extracting from them — you're inviting them into something they'd be proud to be part of.

Most founders avoid making this list because it feels presumptuous to assign people in your life a dollar number. **It isn't presumptuous. It is the work.** Skip it and there is no fundraising plan, only fundraising hope.

---

## If you think you have no one (you do)

Two traps make founders believe the well is dry when it isn't:

**Trap 1 — confusing "family" with "your sphere."** Family is one slice. Your real list is _everyone who'd recognize your name and take your call_: friends, current and former coworkers, every soccer parent from your kids' years, neighbors, your barber, dentist, accountant, college friends, people you follow and who follow you. Most adults know **200–500 people**. You don't need to invent prospects — you need to _catalogue the ones you already have_.

**The 20-minute unlock:** open your **phone contacts** and scroll top to bottom. Every name that would recognize you and take your call → add it below. Don't judge their bank account. Don't guess whether they'd give. Only ask: _do they know me?_ Then do the same with Instagram / Facebook / LinkedIn. Aim for 30 — you'll likely find more.

**Trap 2 — the group ask.** A blast to everyone ("we started a nonprofit, please support us") converts terribly — not because people don't care, but because a message to everyone is a message to no one. The same person who ignored the group email will give to a **personal, one-to-one ask** for a **specific kid** and a **specific amount**. If your only asks so far have been group messages, your list isn't tapped — it's barely been worked.

### Capacity — estimate the _upper bound of one ask_

Not annual, not lifetime — what could they realistically give in a single gift if asked properly?

| Tier  | Range      | Notes                                                                            |
| ----- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A** | $25K–$50K+ | Rare. Usually 1–3 names on the list. Often family or top career contacts.        |
| **B** | $10K–$25K  | Small handful. Could underwrite a full kid's season ($2,500) several times over. |
| **C** | $1K–$10K   | The broader group. The Founders' Circle base.                                    |
| **D** | $100–$1K   | Not on this list — these are sustainers. Keep in a separate broader file.        |

| Score    | Meaning                                                                        |

| **Cool** | Would remember you. Needs a warm reintroduction first.                         |
| **Cold** | Would not recognize you unaided. Needs a mutual-contact intro.                 |

## Working cadence

- **Every Monday:** add 3–5 new names to Step 1. The list is living, not one-shot.
- **Every Friday:** update statuses in Step 2. What moved? What's next?
- **Top 5 get a personal touch every 30 days** until asked.
- **No more than 5 active asks in market at once.** Quality > volume.

---

## What "the ask" looks like (preview for later)

When you ask, you ask **in person or by phone** — never email.

You ask for a **specific amount tied to a specific outcome**.

> _"$2,500 funds a kid for a full year of club soccer at CVFC. We're funding 100 kids in Class of '26 — would you consider being the donor who funds our first kid?"_

You ask **once**, then **stop talking**. Silence is your friend. Whoever speaks first loses.

**Full guide:** `how-to-ask.md` — the mindset, a rehearsable script, and what to say to yes / no / maybe. Read it before your first call.
