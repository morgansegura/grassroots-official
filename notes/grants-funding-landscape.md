# Grants and funding landscape — Grassroots Foundation

Researched May 2026 for a San Diego County-based 501(c)(3) (EIN 35-2822183)
funding need-qualified kids into competitive youth soccer (tuition, kit,
equipment, clinic seats). World Cup 2026 is the campaign tailwind.

This is a working triage doc, not an exhaustive list. Verify every detail at
the source before applying — grant cycles and eligibility move fast.

---

## Apply to these first (highest ROI, San Diego-eligible, time-sensitive)

### 1. LA84 Foundation — Small Grants (rolling) + Large Grants (annual)

- **Geography:** Southern California's 8 counties — explicitly includes
  San Diego.
- **Small grants:** $5,000–$25,000, year-round rolling program.
- **Large grants:** anything above $25,000. Next deadline:
  **Tuesday, July 21, 2026.**
- **Funds:** program costs, sports equipment, facility renovation.
- **Eligibility:** 501(c)(3), in good standing with CA Franchise Tax Board
  via CA Secretary of State.
- **Why it's #1:** the LA84 mandate is literally Southern California youth
  sports. SD-eligible. Mid-five-figure award is realistic for a first
  application. Strong fit with Player Scholarships and Equipment Grants.
- **Action:** verify CA FTB / SoS standing first, then start the small-grant
  app immediately. Stack the large-grant app for the July deadline.
- **Apply:** https://la84.org/grants

### 2. U.S. Soccer Foundation — Soccer Forward grants

- **Soccer Forward** is the U.S. Soccer Federation's funding arm; the
  legacy U.S. Soccer Foundation grants now appear to flow through this and
  the **Innovate to Grow** program (2025–26 Innovate to Grow cycle is
  closed; watch for the next cycle).
- **Safe Places to Play** (synthetic turf, lighting, irrigation, sport
  court): three cycles per year for hard goods; synthetic turf is rolling.
  Not directly a tuition-scholarship vehicle, but a partner-club
  facility win can underwrite Grassroots indirectly via the partner.
- **The Soccer Fund:** see ussoccerfoundation.tfaforms.net/4871632 for the
  current submission form — confirm what it funds in 2026.
- **Action:** subscribe to Soccer Forward and U.S. Soccer Foundation
  newsletters today; watch for the next Innovate to Grow cycle (these
  cycle annually and tend to open mid-summer).
- **Apply:** https://www.ussoccer.com/soccer-forward/innovate-to-grow and
  https://ussoccerfoundation.org

### 3. MLS GO Play Fund — Individual Grants

- **MLS's** annual recreational-soccer access fund. Two grant types:
  Organization Grants (to MLS GO league operators) and **Individual
  Grants** (cover participation fees for individual youth players who
  can't afford them).
- **Fit:** Individual Grants are the closest direct overlap with
  Grassroots' Player Scholarships program. Investigate whether
  Grassroots can be a downstream recipient/distributor for these
  individual grants via SD-area MLS GO leagues, or whether the
  Foundation can partner with SDFC/Wave to access the channel.
- **Action:** contact MLS GO directly; cross-reference with the SDFC
  community team (see #4).
- **Apply:** https://www.mlssoccer.com/mlsgo/play-fund/

### 4. San Diego FC (SDFC) + Right to Dream — local community fund

- SDFC committed an **initial $100,000 to San Diego Foundation** for a
  pooled youth-soccer fund focused on **underserved San Diego County
  communities, removing barriers to participation** — this is verbatim
  the Grassroots use case.
- **Action:** two parallel asks:
  1. Apply through **San Diego Foundation** (see #6) for distributions
     from the SDFC-seeded pool.
  2. Direct outreach to SDFC's community/foundation team. The Right to
     Dream Academy is residential, fully-funded, and pulls only 17 U13
     kids at first — SDFC has strong incentive to point applicants to
     community partners like Grassroots for kids who don't make the
     academy cut. Position Grassroots as the downstream catch.
- **Apply:** https://www.sandiegofc.com — contact community/foundation
  relations directly. There's no public application portal yet.

### 5. Adidas Foundation — Play Collective (World Cup 2026 legacy program)

- **Heads-up — geographic eligibility is tight.** Open only to
  organizations within **50 miles of a FIFA World Cup 2026 host
  stadium**. San Diego is ~125 miles from SoFi Stadium (Inglewood) —
  **likely NOT eligible** on distance grounds. Verify before investing
  time.
- If eligible: **up to $180,000 over 3 years** plus capacity-building
  Community of Practice. Target populations include girls/young women,
  LGBTQIA+ youth, youth experiencing racial/ethnic discrimination, and
  youth with disabilities (≥8 of 21 selected orgs).
- **Action:** confirm SD eligibility via Beyond Sport's RFP page before
  applying. If they interpret "host city region" loosely, this is the
  largest single-pot multi-year opportunity in this list.
- **Apply:** https://beyondsport.smapply.io/prog/play_collective/

## Full landscape by category

### National sports foundations

- **LA84 Foundation** — see above. SD-eligible. Best fit.
- **U.S. Soccer Foundation / Soccer Forward** — see above. Heavily
  focused on facilities; tuition-scholarship pathways are narrower.
- **Laureus Sport for Good USA** — sports-based youth development. 2026
  cycle is open by invitation to orgs in Atlanta, Chicago, New Orleans,
  NYC, plus "other strategic locations." SD is not currently listed.
  **Action:** email LaureusUSA to be added to consideration for the
  next geographic expansion.
- **Beyond Sport** — runs Play Collective (above) and Sport for Good
  Response Fund. Worth a relationship even if not the immediate grantor.
- **Susan Crown Exchange — Youth Sports portfolio** — invitation-based;
  worth getting on their radar via Candid + LinkedIn intros.

### Corporate brand programs

- **Dick's Sporting Goods Foundation — Sports Matter** — $5K–$25K. **2026
  cycle closed (March 31, 2026).** **Now invitation-only** via the Sports
  Matter Community; not an open application. Eligibility: 501(c)(3)
  serving youth in zip codes with ≥15% poverty rate, OR public/charter
  schools with ≥50% free/reduced lunch. **Action:** join the Sports
  Matter Community to be considered for 2027.
  - https://www.sportsmatter.org
- **Nike Community Impact Fund (NCIF)** — **NOT SD-eligible.**
  Geographically restricted to specific Oregon counties + Clark County
  (SW Washington). Skip.
- **Nike — other community grants** — broader Nike giving exists via the
  about.nike.com/community-grants channel. Largely invitation-based; not
  worth optimizing for at our stage.
- **Adidas Free Neighborhood Soccer League** — Adidas + U.S. Soccer
  Foundation joint program creating free leagues in U.S. cities. Worth
  pitching SD as a target city via the U.S. Soccer Foundation
  relationship rather than as a direct Adidas application.

### MLS / pro-league ecosystem

- **MLS GO Play Fund** — see above. Individual Grants are the best fit.
- **MLS NEXT Development Grant Program** — pro-pathway funding;
  upstream from where Grassroots operates. Could become relevant if
  Grassroots ever co-funds a roster spot with an MLS NEXT-participating
  partner club — but partner club is the applicant, not Grassroots.
- **MLS WORKS** — MLS's social-impact arm; primarily channels work
  through MLS clubs rather than direct grants to outside orgs. Worth
  awareness but not a near-term application.

### Local pro clubs (San Diego)

- **San Diego FC (SDFC)** — see above. **Highest-leverage local
  pro-club relationship.** Build it deliberately.
- **San Diego Wave FC — Wave FC Impact Fund** — community-impact fund
  exists; not a published grant portal. Engage via partnership
  proposal, not a cold app. Wave's 2026 activity centers on free
  clinics + community appearances. **Action:** propose Grassroots as a
  scholarship co-funder for Wave-aligned girls' programming
  (NWSL-pipeline narrative + Class of '26 story arc).
- **Possible adjacent (LA-region but SD-proximate):** LA84 covers all
  of this; LA Galaxy / LAFC / Angel City foundations are likely LA-county
  scoped — confirm before pursuing.

### San Diego County foundations

- **San Diego Foundation** — apply at
  https://www.sdfoundation.org/nonprofits/apply-for-a-grant. Multiple
  funds; the **SDFC-seeded youth athletes pool** is the most directly
  relevant. Eligibility: 501(c)(3) in/serving San Diego County.
- **Conrad Prebys Foundation** — 2026 deployed ~$78M across SD. One of
  four pillars is **Youth Success** (alongside arts, health, medical
  research). Not a fast-turn open-app shop; relationship-driven. **Action:**
  request a discovery conversation via prebysfdn.org once Grassroots has
  a polished pitch deck.
- **Price Philanthropies / Price Charities** — part of "United for San
  Diego" with Prebys + SDF. Focus is food/housing/healthcare per the
  2026 announcement, less directly youth sports — but adjacent giving
  exists. Relationship-driven.
- **Jewish Community Foundation San Diego (JCF SD)** — DAF-heavy. Useful
  channel for individual donor advisors to direct gifts; not a direct
  grantmaker for sports.
- **The Parker Foundation, Las Patronas, Rancho Santa Fe Foundation,
  Qualcomm Foundation** — all worth Candid lookups for current cycles.
  Several are family-foundation style with relationship-based applications.

### Equipment / in-kind

- **Good Sports** — donates new gear (cleats, balls, uniforms, training
  equipment) to high-need youth programs nationwide. **501(c)(3) NOT
  required**, but 10% admin fee applies. Direct fit with Grassroots'
  **Kit & Uniform Grants** and **Equipment Grants** programs — could
  effectively underwrite a season of kits at near-zero hard cost.
  - https://www.goodsports.org

### Corporate giving (open programs)

- **Allstate Foundation** — Youth Empowerment focus, accepts LOIs from
  U.S. nonprofits (grants@allstate.com). Larger-scale than our stage,
  but a LOI sent now starts the clock for future cycles.
- **Bank of America Foundation** — economic mobility focus + sports
  sponsorships. Local BofA market presidents have discretion; SD market
  contact is worth establishing.
- **Wells Fargo Foundation** — invitation-process; financial opportunity
  / housing / small business priorities. Less direct fit unless framed
  around economic mobility.

### Federal (longer-horizon)

These are heavier-lift applications. Park them for Year 2.

- **OJJDP (Office of Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention)** —
  Title V community-based prevention. Has historically funded sports-based
  prevention; check current FY26 NOFOs.
- **21st Century Community Learning Centers (21st CCLC)** — after-school
  programming. Run through state ed departments. Soccer-as-after-school
  is in scope.
- **USDA Summer EBT / After-School Snack** — adjacent to programming
  but not direct.

## Eligibility traps to watch

- **CA Franchise Tax Board standing** — multiple CA grantmakers require
  it. Verify before any application. If lapsed, fix it via
  ftb.ca.gov/businesses before naming the foundation in a draft.
- **Geographic scope** — Nike NCIF (Oregon only), Play Collective
  (50 miles of host stadium), Laureus (named cities only),
  LA-region pro-club foundations (likely LA County only). Read the
  RFP geography line before drafting anything.
- **Invitation-only programs** — Sports Matter, Laureus USA, many corporate
  programs. The play is to get on their radar (newsletter subscriptions,
  LOIs, LinkedIn introductions via board/advisors) so the invitation
  shows up.
- **Soccer-program inputs vs. soccer-program operators** — many
  sport-for-good funders fund the _operator_ of a soccer program.
  Grassroots is the _funder of the kid_, not the operator. Pitch
  carefully: emphasize the partner-club operator relationship, the
  whole-cost-stack coverage (tuition + kit + travel), and the
  measurable kid-level outcomes Grassroots tracks. Some funders will
  prefer to fund the partner club directly; in that case, the play is
  to be the _intake/eligibility/scholarship engine_ the partner club
  uses, with Grassroots as the dollar-distribution mechanism.
- **501(c)(3) age and audit** — some grantmakers require 1–2 years of
  audited financials. As a young org, surface the strategic plan,
  founding governance, and Candid Bronze→Platinum trajectory to compensate.

## Adjacent revenue plays (not grants but real money)

- **Givebutter individual giving** — already in the stack. Maximize
  founding-donor recurring tier conversions before chasing grants.
- **DAF inbound (Chariot, DAFpay widget)** — high-net-worth donors
  often hold DAF balances they want to deploy. A DAF widget on the
  donate page surfaces those dollars.
- **Employer match (Double the Donation widget)** — doubles inbound
  individual gifts at ~zero marginal cost.
- **PayPal Giving Fund** — already enrolled. Surfaces Grassroots in
  PayPal donor checkout flows.
- **Givebutter peer-to-peer fundraising** — built-in. Recruit
  founding donors to run their own micro-campaigns (one kid per
  donor's social graph).
- **Corporate sponsor matching against the Class of '26 roster** —
  $2,500 = one kid one season. Pitch SD employers (Petco HQ, Qualcomm,
  Sycuan, Viejas, Sempra, Illumina) to fund 5–25 named roster spots
  with co-branding.
