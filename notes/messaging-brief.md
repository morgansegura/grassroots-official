# Messaging brief

How Grassroots Foundation talks about itself, grounded in what kids and donors
actually respond to rather than what sounds good. Every page, ad, and email
follows this. Written September 22, 2026.

## What the research says

**Kids play for fun and friends.** In the Aspen Institute's national youth
survey (almost 4,000 kids), the top reasons current players give are having fun
(48%) and playing with friends (47%). College scholarships are a distant 12%.
The average age kids quit is about 13. ([Project Play, 15 key
findings](https://projectplay.org/news/aspen-institute-national-survey-of-youth-and-sports-15-key-findings))

**Cost is a real barrier, and it falls on lower-income kids.** Low-income youth
make up 34% of kids who have never played a sport; high-income youth, 8%.
Low-income players rely on school sports; high-income players are
concentrated in private clubs and travel teams. (Same source.)

**Donors give to a specific, believable outcome.** Messages that work name a
specific need, say what the gift will do, and make giving easy. Trust comes
from knowing where the money goes. ([Bloomerang, What Motivates Donors in
2026](https://bloomerang.com/blog/2026-giving-report-donor-motivation-and-charitable-giving-trends))

**Talk about what the gift does, in a welcoming voice about the reader.**
NextAfter found communal, conversational copy that centers the reader lifted
conversions 56% over organization-centered copy. Telling donors what their gift
will do beats telling them what they get, and value-proposition copy on a
monthly-gift page lifted conversions 31.5%. ([NextAfter
experiments](https://www.nextafter.com/all-experiments/))

## The message

Lead with kids playing the game they love, with their friends, on a real team.
Say plainly what a gift covers. Keep the cost of club soccer as a supporting
fact, stated calmly: it is the reason to give, not the headline.

- **Hopeful, never pity.** No sad scenes: no one "sitting out", "priced out",
  "watching from the sideline", or facing a "crisis".
- **Nobody is the villain.** Not clubs, coaches, families, or "the system". We
  add to what clubs already do.
- **The kid is the subject; the foundation is the proof.** We are how the help
  gets there, and we show our work (990, EIN, Candid).
- **Full sentences, adult and warm.** No slogans, no punchy fragments, no em
  dashes.

## The numbers we use

Only these, and only as stated.

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Pair of cleats | $60 to $300 |
| A season's kit | $300 to $600 |
| A season of tuition | $2,500 at Chula Vista FC, up to $4,500 at other clubs |

**Monthly giving, honestly** (matches the Zeffy amounts; $100 is the default):

| Monthly | A year | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $25 | $300 | Cleats for a growing player |
| $50 | $600 | A full season's kit |
| $100 | $1,200 | About half a season of tuition at Chula Vista FC |
| $250 | $3,000 | A full season of tuition at Chula Vista FC |

Never say $50 a month funds a kid's season or year. Never price a whole team at
$25,000; a 15-player team's tuition runs $37,500 to $67,500.
