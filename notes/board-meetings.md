# How to Run Your Board

> How often you meet, who does what, what you talk about, what to measure, and
> how to keep clean minutes. Built for a small founding board. Pairs with
> nonprofit-startup-guide.md Ch. 9 (founding-meeting + minutes templates).

## How often you meet

- **Legal floor:** quarterly (every 90 days).
- **Recommended while building (Year 1):** every other month (6/year). A new board needs more touchpoints; drop to quarterly once stable.
- **Plus one Annual Meeting** a year (can be one of the regulars) to approve the prior year, approve the budget, and elect/renew officers.
- Put all of next year's dates on the calendar at once.

## How long

A regular meeting runs **60–90 minutes.** If it's running long, you're doing day-to-day operations work that belongs outside the meeting.

## Who does what

- **President (Morgan)** — sets the agenda, runs the meeting, keeps time. Gives the founder/ED report.
- **Treasurer (CFO)** — presents the financials: cash on hand, money in vs. budget, concerns.
- **Secretary** — takes minutes, sends the agenda + prior minutes a week ahead, keeps the records.
- **All directors** — show up prepared, ask questions, vote, help with an intro or an ask.

## What you talk about (regular-meeting agenda)

Send this agenda + last meeting's minutes one week ahead. Standard order:

1. **Call to order** — note time, who's present, confirm quorum (majority of directors).
2. **Approve last meeting's minutes** — quick vote.
3. **Founder / ED report (Morgan)** — state of the org: wins, challenges, what you need from the board.
4. **Treasurer's report** — financials vs. budget, cash position.
5. **The dashboard** — review the measures below.
6. **Decisions needing a vote** — budget items, policies, big contracts, new directors.
7. **Fundraising & relationships** — who's cultivating whom; intros the board can make.
8. **Action items** — who owns what before next meeting.
9. **Confirm next meeting · adjourn.**

## What to measure (your board dashboard)

Review the same numbers every meeting so the board sees the trend:

- **Dollars raised** — this period and year-to-date, vs. goal.
- **Kids funded** to date (the mission metric).
- **Cash on hand** and runway.
- **Donor pipeline** — active prospects, asks made this period.
- **Grants** — submitted, pending, won/declined.
- **Compliance** — anything due (filings, renewals) and its status.

Keep it to one page. The board watches the trend and the big decisions; it doesn't run the day-to-day.

## Minutes (the legal record)

- **Keep them short — 1–2 pages.** Minutes record _decisions_, not a transcript.
- For each vote: the motion, who moved and seconded, the result (e.g., "Approved 4–0").
- Capture: date, who was present/absent, quorum confirmed, decisions made, action items, time adjourned.
- The Secretary drafts within a week, the board approves them at the next meeting, the Secretary signs and files them.
- Minutes are kept **permanently** — they're what prove the board acted properly if a funder or the IRS ever asks.

## The annual rhythm

- **Annual Meeting:** approve the prior year, approve the new budget, elect/renew officers.
- **Every May (compliance window):** confirm 990-N, RRF-1, etc. are filed (see governance-tracker.md).
- **Once a year:** each director signs a conflict-of-interest disclosure.

## Your first one: the founding / reorganizing meeting

Once your 3rd director says yes, the _first_ meeting is special: it seats the new directors, re-adopts the bylaws with everyone signing, adopts the five required policies, and elects officers. Use the agenda + minutes templates in **nonprofit-startup-guide.md Ch. 9**. After that, regular meetings follow the agenda above.
